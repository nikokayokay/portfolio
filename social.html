“Adapt or die.” That line from Moneyball, delivered by Brad Pitt, wasn’t really about baseball. It was about how industries change when better ways of thinking show up. Sports analytics is going through that shift again. Most data is still presented like it was 15 years ago. Static tables, cluttered dashboards, and tools that feel more like spreadsheets than actual products. But newer platforms like Real Sports app, Databallr, and Draftballr are starting to challenge that by making data more visual and interactive. That’s the idea behind what I’ve been building. Moneyballr (working title) is an MLB analytics platform focused on combining traditional stats with modern visualization: • Live-updating MLB data • Advanced metrics inspired by FanGraphs • Interactive strike zone breakdowns for every pitch • Player dashboards, including a full profile for Sal Stewart The goal is simple: make baseball data easier to understand and actually usable in real time. Still early, but I’m testing whether this approach holds up beyond just looking clean. If you’ve used analytics tools recently, what actually makes one worth coming back to?
